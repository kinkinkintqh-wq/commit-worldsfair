# Commit — project presentation

Voice: Andrew Multilingual Neural. AI-generated narration.

## Tomorrow’s capacity. Committed today.

Hi, I’m Hoikin, a solo founder based in Hong Kong. I’m building Commit: a forward market for agent capacity. It starts with a simple question: can an agent reserve the service it will need tomorrow, today?

## A deadline needs a promise.

Imagine an agent preparing a scheduled report tomorrow morning. It can discover a data service and pay for a call. But that does not guarantee tomorrow’s capacity, price, time window, or what happens if delivery fails. A future deadline needs a clear delivery agreement.

## From listing to commitment.

This question came from working on agent service delivery. Listing a service was only the beginning. Making a dependable delivery promise, and checking what actually happened afterward, was the harder problem. Commit adds a reservation layer for that future work.

## Agree. Reserve. Deliver.

The buyer gets a quote with quantity, price, a future window and delivery criteria. Confirmation reserves a participating provider’s declared quota. Escrow funds the order. During the booked window, the provider executes. Verification then determines whether to pay the provider or refund the buyer.

## One record. Both outcomes.

In the prototype, three retrieval units cost fifteen cents. The quote does not lock capacity; confirmation does. A fixed-corpus provider returns cited content. Checks cover quantity, observed latency, sources and integrity. A passing order settles. A failing order returns the full amount. Both outcomes remain on one record.

## A visible money trail.

The Solana Devnet adapter uses Circle’s test U S D C, separate reservation vaults and memos binding payment to terms. Buyer-signed deposits, a verified provider payout and a separate full refund of fifteen cents are confirmed. The linked receipts were independently checked on Devnet. Custody, the provider and verification are project-operated. The product captures shown here are labeled simulations.

## Start with repeat workflows.

Our first target is developers running repeated agent workflows with measurable deadlines. We plan to interview ten workflow operators and five providers, then recruit up to two external providers for a controlled pilot. We will measure delivery, refunds and repeat bookings. These are targets: there are no customers, revenue, external pilots or funding today.

## Reserve the next task.

A fee on fulfilled reservations or provider subscriptions is a business model hypothesis, not validated pricing. Commit starts with measurable delivery, clear money flows and an honest capacity commitment. The goal is to make future agent services easier to plan and compare. Tomorrow’s capacity. Committed today.
